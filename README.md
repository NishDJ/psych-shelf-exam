# Psychiatry Clerkship Shelf Exam Study Guide

## 📚 Welcome, Medical Students!

This comprehensive resource is designed to help you master the content for your Psychiatry clerkship shelf exam. The material is organized by major diagnostic categories and key topics that are frequently tested.

## 🎯 How to Use This Resource

1. **Start with high-yield topics** - Focus on mood disorders, psychotic disorders, and emergency psychiatry first
2. **Use active recall** - Test yourself on diagnostic criteria, treatment approaches, and clinical scenarios
3. **Review pharmacology regularly** - Psychopharmacology is heavily tested
4. **Practice clinical reasoning** - Most questions are case-based, not just memorization
5. **Know the numbers** - Be familiar with epidemiology, age of onset, and treatment timelines

## 📋 Content Organization

### Core Psychiatric Disorders

**01-Mood-Disorders** (~15-20% of exam)
- Major Depressive Disorder
- Bipolar Disorder (Type I & II)
- Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia)
- Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder
- Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder

**02-Psychotic-Disorders** (~10-15% of exam)
- Schizophrenia
- Schizoaffective Disorder
- Delusional Disorder
- Brief Psychotic Disorder
- Schizophreniform Disorder

**03-Anxiety-Disorders** (~10-12% of exam)
- Generalized Anxiety Disorder
- Panic Disorder
- Social Anxiety Disorder
- Specific Phobias
- Separation Anxiety Disorder

**04-Trauma-and-Stressor-Related-Disorders** (~8-10% of exam)
- Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
- Acute Stress Disorder
- Adjustment Disorders
- Reactive Attachment Disorder

**05-Substance-Use-Disorders** (~10-12% of exam)
- Alcohol Use Disorder
- Opioid Use Disorder
- Stimulant Use Disorders
- Cannabis, Benzodiazepines, and Others
- Intoxication and Withdrawal Syndromes

**06-Personality-Disorders** (~5-8% of exam)

- Cluster B: Antisocial, Borderline, Histrionic, Narcissistic
- Cluster C: Avoidant, Dependent, Obsessive-Compulsive

**07-Neurocognitive-Disorders** (~8-10% of exam)
- Delirium
- Major and Mild Neurocognitive Disorders
- Alzheimer's Disease
- Vascular Dementia
- Lewy Body Dementia

### Special Populations

**09-Geriatric-Psychiatry** (~5-7% of exam)

## 🔥 High-Yield Topics to Master

1. **Diagnostic Criteria** - Know DSM-5 criteria for major disorders
2. **First-line Treatments** - Pharmacologic and psychotherapeutic
3. **Side Effects** - Especially for antipsychotics and mood stabilizers
4. **Suicide Risk Factors** - Assessment and management
5. **Delirium vs. Dementia** - Key distinguishing features
6. **Substance Withdrawal** - Timelines and management
7. **Child Psychiatry** - ADHD, autism, developmental milestones
8. **Capacity Assessment** - When and how to assess
9. **Serotonin Syndrome** - Recognition and management
10. **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome** - Recognition and management

## 📖 Study Tips

### Timeline Recommendations
- **6+ weeks before exam**: Work through all categories systematically
- **4 weeks before**: Focus on weak areas, do practice questions
- **2 weeks before**: Review high-yield topics, pharmacology
- **1 week before**: Practice questions, review mnemonics, final review

### Active Learning Strategies
- Create flashcards for diagnostic criteria
- Practice writing out treatment algorithms
- Work through clinical vignettes daily
- Teach concepts to peers
- Make comparison charts (e.g., delirium vs. dementia)

### Question Practice
- Aim for 50-100 practice questions per day in final 2 weeks
- Review explanations for ALL questions (right and wrong)
- Focus on understanding reasoning, not just memorizing answers
- Time yourself to simulate exam conditions

## 🎓 Clinical Pearls

- **Most psychiatric diagnoses require symptoms to cause distress or impairment**
- **Rule out medical causes first** - Many conditions can mimic psychiatric disorders
- **Time course matters** - Know duration criteria for diagnoses
- **Substance use is common** - Always consider in differential
- **Suicidality must be assessed** - Especially with mood disorders
- **Children are not just small adults** - Different presentations and considerations
- **Side effects can be serious** - Know when to stop medications
