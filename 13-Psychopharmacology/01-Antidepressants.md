# Antidepressants

## 🎯 Overview
Antidepressants are first-line medications for Major Depressive Disorder, anxiety disorders, and several other conditions. Understanding their mechanisms, side effects, and selection criteria is HIGH-YIELD for the shelf exam.

## 📚 Classification

1. **SSRIs** (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors) - First-line
2. **SNRIs** (Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors) - First-line
3. **TCAs** (Tricyclic Antidepressants) - Second-line
4. **MAOIs** (Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors) - Third-line
5. **Atypical Antidepressants** - Various mechanisms

---

## 1. SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors)

### Mechanism of Action
- **Block serotonin reuptake** at presynaptic terminal
- Increases serotonin in synaptic cleft
- Downregulation of receptors over time (why takes 2-4 weeks to work)

### Common SSRIs

| Drug | Dose Range | Half-Life | Key Features |
|------|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Fluoxetine** (Prozac) | 20-80 mg | 4-6 days (longest) | Long half-life; activating; fewer withdrawal symptoms |
| **Sertraline** (Zoloft) | 50-200 mg | 26 hours | Most GI side effects; safe in pregnancy |
| **Paroxetine** (Paxil) | 20-50 mg | 21 hours | Most anticholinergic; worst withdrawal; avoid in pregnancy |
| **Citalopram** (Celexa) | 20-40 mg MAX | 35 hours | QTc prolongation risk; max 20 mg if >60 yo |
| **Escitalopram** (Lexapro) | 10-20 mg | 27-32 hours | Fewest drug interactions; well-tolerated |
| **Fluvoxamine** (Luvox) | 100-300 mg | 15 hours | OCD indication; CYP450 inhibitor |

### Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder** (first-line)
- **Anxiety disorders**: GAD, Panic, Social Anxiety, OCD
- **PTSD**
- **Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder** (fluoxetine, sertraline)
- **Bulimia Nervosa** (fluoxetine)
- **Premature ejaculation** (off-label: paroxetine, sertraline)

### Side Effects

#### Common (10-30%)
- **Sexual dysfunction** (60-70%): Decreased libido, anorgasmia, erectile dysfunction
- **GI upset**: Nausea, diarrhea (usually transient, first 1-2 weeks)
- **Headache**
- **Insomnia or sedation** (varies by drug and person)
- **Weight gain** (long-term, more with paroxetine)
- **Diaphoresis** (sweating)

#### Serious/Rare
- **Serotonin syndrome** (especially with multiple serotonergic drugs)
- **Hyponatremia** (SIADH, especially in elderly)
- **GI bleeding** (decreases platelet aggregation; risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants)
- **Increased suicidal ideation** (Black Box Warning: age <25, especially first 4 weeks)
- **QTc prolongation** (citalopram >40 mg/day)
- **Bone loss** (long-term use)

#### Discontinuation Syndrome
Occurs with abrupt cessation (especially paroxetine, fluvoxamine)
**Symptoms**: FINISH
- **F**lu-like symptoms
- **I**nsomnia
- **N**ausea
- **I**mbalance (dizziness)
- **S**ensory disturbances (paresthesias, "brain zaps")
- **H**ypersousal (anxiety, agitation)

**Prevention**: Taper slowly over weeks to months

### Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 inhibitors**: Fluoxetine, Fluvoxamine, Paroxetine (strong inhibitors)
  - Can increase levels of other medications metabolized by CYP2D6, CYP3A4
- **Avoid with MAOIs**: 2-week washout (5 weeks for fluoxetine)
- **Caution with other serotonergic drugs**: TCAs, triptans, tramadol, St. John's Wort

### Special Considerations
**Pregnancy**:
- **Preferred**: Sertraline (most data, considered safest)
- **Avoid**: Paroxetine (cardiac defects)
- All SSRIs: Small risk of PPHN (persistent pulmonary hypertension of newborn), neonatal adaptation syndrome

**Elderly**: Start low, go slow; monitor sodium (SIADH risk)

### Clinical Pearls
✓ **Take 2-4 weeks** for initial effect, **6-8 weeks** for full effect
✓ **Sexual side effects** are #1 reason for non-adherence (60-70% incidence)
✓ **Fluoxetine** has longest half-life → fewest withdrawal symptoms
✓ **Paroxetine** has worst withdrawal, most anticholinergic
✓ **Sertraline** most GI side effects initially, but safest in pregnancy
✓ **Citalopram** max dose 40 mg (QTc prolongation), 20 mg if >60 years old
✓ **Escitalopram** (Lexapro) is S-enantiomer of citalopram → fewer side effects

## 2. SNRIs (Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors)

### Mechanism of Action
- Block reuptake of both **serotonin AND norepinephrine**
- Similar efficacy to SSRIs for depression
- May be better for pain syndromes (norepinephrine component)

### Common SNRIs

| Drug | Dose Range | Key Features |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| **Venlafaxine** (Effexor) | 75-225 mg | Dose-dependent: Low dose = SSRI, High dose = SNRI; can increase BP |
| **Desvenlafaxine** (Pristiq) | 50 mg | Active metabolite of venlafaxine; linear dosing |
| **Duloxetine** (Cymbalta) | 30-60 mg | FDA approved for chronic pain, fibromyalgia, diabetic neuropathy |
| **Levomilnacipran** (Fetzima) | 40-120 mg | More norepinephrine than serotonin reuptake |

### Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder**
- **Generalized Anxiety Disorder**
- **Chronic pain syndromes**: Fibromyalgia, diabetic neuropathy (duloxetine)
- **Stress urinary incontinence** (duloxetine)

### Side Effects
Similar to SSRIs, PLUS:
- **Hypertension** (dose-dependent, especially venlafaxine >150 mg)
- **Increased heart rate**
- **More withdrawal symptoms** than SSRIs (especially venlafaxine)
- **Urinary hesitancy** (norepinephrine effect)

### Clinical Pearls
✓ **Monitor blood pressure** especially at higher doses
✓ **Duloxetine** good choice if comorbid chronic pain
✓ **Withdrawal** can be more severe than SSRIs (taper carefully)
✓ **May be effective** when SSRI fails

## 3. TCAs (Tricyclic Antidepressants)

### Mechanism of Action
- Block reuptake of serotonin and norepinephrine
- Also block: H1 (histamine), α1-adrenergic, muscarinic (anticholinergic)
- Side effects limit use (second-line)

### Common TCAs

| Drug | Type | Key Features |
|------|------|--------------|
| **Amitriptyline** | Tertiary amine | Most sedating, anticholinergic; good for chronic pain, migraine prophylaxis |
| **Nortriptyline** | Secondary amine | Less sedating than amitriptyline; good for elderly |
| **Imipramine** | Tertiary amine | Enuresis in children; panic disorder |
| **Desipramine** | Secondary amine | Least anticholinergic; ADHD (off-label) |
| **Clomipramine** | Tertiary amine | Most serotonergic; OCD (first-line TCA) |
| **Doxepin** | Tertiary amine | Very sedating; low dose for insomnia |

**Pattern**: **Tertiary amines** (ending in -triptyline) are more sedating and anticholinergic

### Indications
- **MDD** (second-line, after SSRIs/SNRIs fail)
- **Chronic pain syndromes**: Neuropathic pain, migraines
- **OCD** (clomipramine)
- **Enuresis** (imipramine)
- **Insomnia** (doxepin 3-6 mg)

### Side Effects

#### Anticholinergic (most problematic)
**Mnemonic: Blind as a bat, Mad as a hatter, Red as a beet, Hot as a hare, Dry as a bone, The bowel and bladder lose their tone, The heart runs alone**

- **Dry mouth**
- **Blurred vision** (mydriasis)
- **Constipation**
- **Urinary retention**
- **Confusion** (especially in elderly)
- **Tachycardia**

#### Antihistaminic
- **Sedation**
- **Weight gain**

#### Alpha-1 Blockade
- **Orthostatic hypotension** (major concern in elderly → falls)

#### Cardiac
- **QTc prolongation**
- **Arrhythmias**
- **LETHAL in overdose** (cardiotoxicity)

#### Other
- **Seizure threshold lowering**
- **Sexual dysfunction**
- **Weight gain**

### Contraindications
- **Recent MI** (arrhythmia risk)
- **Bundle branch block**
- **Narrow-angle glaucoma** (anticholinergic)
- **Urinary retention** (BPH)

### Overdose
**Very dangerous** (lethal):
- **Cardiac arrhythmias** (widened QRS)
- **Seizures**
- **Anticholinergic toxidrome**
- **Treatment**: Sodium bicarbonate (for QRS widening), activated charcoal, supportive care

### Clinical Pearls
✓ **Second-line** for depression (due to side effects)
✓ **Good for chronic pain** and migraines (amitriptyline, nortriptyline)
✓ **Nortriptyline** better tolerated in elderly than amitriptyline
✓ **Lethal in overdose** → avoid in suicidal patients
✓ **Anticholinergic side effects** limit tolerability
✓ **Baseline ECG** recommended before starting

## 4. MAOIs (Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors)

### Mechanism of Action
- Irreversibly inhibit monoamine oxidase enzyme
- Increases synaptic levels of serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine
- Very effective but dangerous side effects → third-line

### Common MAOIs

| Drug | Type | Key Features |
|------|------|--------------|
| **Phenelzine** (Nardil) | Irreversible, non-selective | Most commonly used |
| **Tranylcypromine** (Parnate) | Irreversible, non-selective | More activating |
| **Selegiline** (Emsam) | Irreversible MAO-B selective (low dose) | Transdermal patch; at low dose no diet restriction |
| **Isocarboxazid** (Marplan) | Irreversible, non-selective | Less commonly used |

### Indications
- **Atypical depression** (most effective)
- **Treatment-resistant depression**
- **Panic disorder**
- **Social anxiety disorder**

### Side Effects
- **Orthostatic hypotension** (most common)
- **Weight gain**
- **Sexual dysfunction**
- **Insomnia**
- **Hypertensive crisis** (with tyramine-containing foods)
- **Serotonin syndrome** (with serotonergic drugs)

### MAOI Diet (Tyramine Restriction)
**Must avoid tyramine-rich foods** → Hypertensive crisis

**Avoid**:
- **Aged cheeses** (cheddar, blue cheese, etc.)
- **Cured/smoked meats** (salami, pepperoni, sausage)
- **Fermented foods** (sauerkraut, kimchi, soy sauce)
- **Draft beer**, red wine
- **Fava beans**
- **Yeast extracts** (Marmite)

**Hypertensive crisis symptoms**: Severe headache, palpitations, chest pain, sweating
**Treatment**: Phentolamine (alpha-blocker)

### Drug Interactions (CRITICAL)
- **SSRIs/SNRIs**: Must wait **2 weeks** after stopping MAOI before starting SSRI (5 weeks if fluoxetine)
- **Meperidine (Demerol)**: Absolutely contraindicated (serotonin syndrome)
- **Tramadol**: Contraindicated
- **Sympathomimetics**: Decongestants, stimulants (hypertensive crisis)
- **Dextromethorphan**: Avoid (serotonin syndrome)

### Clinical Pearls
✓ **Most effective for atypical depression** (hypersomnia, increased appetite, rejection sensitivity)
✓ **Third-line** due to diet restrictions and drug interactions
✓ **Irreversible inhibition** → effects last ~2 weeks after stopping (must wait before starting other antidepressant)
✓ **Selegiline patch** (Emsam) at 6 mg/24hr doesn't require diet restriction
✓ Know **tyramine-rich foods** for exam
✓ **Hypertensive crisis** is classic exam question

## 5. Atypical Antidepressants

### Bupropion (Wellbutrin)

**Mechanism**: Inhibits dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake

**Indications**:
- MDD (especially with fatigue, low energy)
- **Smoking cessation** (Zyban)
- ADHD (off-label)
- **Sexual dysfunction** from SSRIs (add-on or switch)

**Pros**:
- **NO sexual side effects**
- **No weight gain** (may cause weight loss)
- **Activating** (good for fatigue)

**Cons**:
- **Lowers seizure threshold** (dose-dependent)
- **Contraindicated**: Seizure disorder, eating disorders (purging → electrolyte abnormalities → seizures)
- Can cause insomnia, anxiety, agitation
- Not effective for anxiety

**Dosing**: 150-450 mg/day (max 450 mg to minimize seizure risk)
- **Immediate release**: Max 150 mg per dose
- **SR (sustained release)**: Max 200 mg per dose
- **XL (extended release)**: Max 450 mg once daily

**Clinical Pearls**:
✓ **Best choice if sexual side effects concern**
✓ **Also helps with smoking cessation**
✓ **Avoid in seizure disorders or eating disorders**
✓ **Not good for anxiety** (can worsen)

---

### Mirtazapine (Remeron)

**Mechanism**: α2-antagonist → increases NE and serotonin; also 5-HT2, 5-HT3, H1 antagonist

**Indications**:
- MDD (especially with insomnia, poor appetite)
- Elderly with depression

**Pros**:
- **Sedating** → good for insomnia
- **Increases appetite** → good for weight loss/anorexia
- Minimal sexual side effects
- Works faster than SSRIs (sometimes)

**Cons**:
- **Significant weight gain**
- **Sedation** (especially lower doses; less at higher doses - paradoxical)
- Increased cholesterol

**Dosing**: 15-45 mg/day (at bedtime)

**Clinical Pearls**:
✓ **"California rocket fuel"** = Mirtazapine + Venlafaxine (both NE/serotonin)
✓ **Lower doses more sedating** (antihistamine effect predominates)
✓ **Great for insomnia and poor appetite**
✓ **Not good if weight gain is concern**

---

### Trazodone (Desyrel)

**Mechanism**: Serotonin antagonist and reuptake inhibitor (SARI)

**Indications**:
- MDD (300-400 mg/day)
- **Insomnia** (50-100 mg) - most common use

**Pros**:
- **Sedating** → widely used for insomnia
- Minimal sexual side effects

**Cons**:
- **Orthostatic hypotension**
- **Priapism** (rare but important, 1 in 6000)
- **Sedation**

**Dosing**:
- Depression: 150-400 mg/day
- Insomnia: 50-100 mg at bedtime

**Clinical Pearls**:
✓ **Most commonly used for insomnia** (off-label)
✓ **Priapism** is classic side effect (warn male patients)
✓ Less effective than SSRIs for depression (not first-line)

---

### Vilazodone (Viibryd) & Vortioxetine (Trintellix)

**Mechanism**: SSRI + 5-HT1A partial agonist

**Key features**:
- Newer agents
- May have **less sexual dysfunction** than SSRIs
- Similar efficacy to SSRIs
- Vortioxetine may help with cognitive symptoms

**Dosing**:
- Vilazodone: 10-40 mg with food
- Vortioxetine: 5-20 mg

## 🔥 Serotonin Syndrome

### Cause
Excess serotonergic activity (multiple serotonergic drugs, high doses)

### Classic Triad
1. **Autonomic instability**: Hyperthermia, tachycardia, BP changes, diaphoresis
2. **Neuromuscular hyperactivity**: Clonus (spontaneous or inducible), hyperreflexia, tremor, rigidity
3. **Mental status changes**: Agitation, confusion, coma

### Management
1. **Stop offending agent(s)**
2. **Supportive care**: Cooling, fluids, BP management
3. **Benzodiazepines**: For agitation
4. **Cyproheptadine**: Serotonin antagonist (if severe)

### Prevention
- Avoid combining multiple serotonergic agents
- Wait appropriate washout period when switching (especially MAOIs)

## 📚 Must-Know for Shelf Exam

✓ **SSRIs first-line** for MDD and anxiety disorders
✓ **Takes 2-4 weeks** to start working, **6-8 weeks** for full effect
✓ **Sexual dysfunction** most common SSRI side effect (60-70%)
✓ **Black Box Warning**: Increased suicidal ideation age <25
✓ **Serotonin syndrome**: Triad of autonomic, neuromuscular, mental status changes
✓ **Bupropion**: No sexual side effects; contraindicated in seizures, eating disorders
✓ **Mirtazapine**: Sedating, increases appetite and weight
✓ **TCAs**: Lethal in overdose (cardiac toxicity); anticholinergic side effects
✓ **MAOIs**: Tyramine diet; most effective for atypical depression
✓ **Discontinuation syndrome**: Taper to avoid (especially paroxetine, venlafaxine)
✓ **Citalopram**: Max 40 mg (QTc prolongation)
✓ **Sertraline**: Safest in pregnancy
✓ **Paroxetine**: Avoid in pregnancy (cardiac defects)
