# Antipsychotics

## 🎯 Overview
Antipsychotics are medications used primarily for psychotic disorders (schizophrenia, bipolar mania, psychotic depression). Understanding the side effect profiles is CRITICAL for the shelf exam.

## 📚 Classification

### First-Generation (Typical) Antipsychotics
- **High potency**: Haloperidol, Fluphenazine
- **Low potency**: Chlorpromazine, Thioridazine

### Second-Generation (Atypical) Antipsychotics
- Risperidone, Olanzapine, Quetiapine, Aripiprazole, Ziprasidone, Lurasidone, Clozapine (special), Paliperidone, Asenapine, Iloperidone

---

## ⚡ Mechanism of Action

### First-Generation (Typical)
- **D2 (dopamine) receptor antagonists**
- Block dopamine in all pathways:
  - Mesolimbic (therapeutic - positive symptoms)
  - Mesocortical (can worsen negative symptoms)
  - Nigrostriatal (causes EPS)
  - Tuberoinfundibular (causes hyperprolactinemia)

### Second-Generation (Atypical)
- **D2 + 5-HT2A (serotonin) antagonists**
- "Looser" D2 binding (less EPS)
- More receptor activity beyond dopamine
- Better for negative symptoms

---

## 🏥 First-Generation (Typical) Antipsychotics

### High-Potency Typicals

#### Haloperidol (Haldol)

**Characteristics**:
- Most commonly used typical antipsychotic
- Available PO, IM, IV, long-acting injectable (decanoate)
- Very effective for acute agitation

**Pros**:
- **Minimal sedation** (compared to low-potency)
- Minimal anticholinergic effects
- Minimal hypotension
- Fast-acting IM for acute agitation

**Cons**:
- **HIGH risk of EPS** (highest of all antipsychotics)
- High risk of tardive dyskinesia
- QTc prolongation (especially IV)
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome risk

**Dosing**:
- Acute agitation: 2-5 mg IM q30min PRN
- Maintenance: 5-20 mg/day
- Decanoate: 50-200 mg IM q4 weeks

**Indications**:
- Schizophrenia
- **Acute agitation** (IM)
- Mania
- Tourette's syndrome
- Delirium (controversial - use cautiously)

#### Fluphenazine (Prolixin)

**Characteristics**:
- Similar to haloperidol
- Long-acting injectable available (decanoate)

**Dosing**: 2.5-10 mg/day PO; 12.5-50 mg IM q2-4 weeks (decanoate)

### Low-Potency Typicals

#### Chlorpromazine (Thorazine)

**Characteristics**:
- First antipsychotic developed (1950s)
- "Dirty drug" - blocks many receptors

**Pros**:
- More sedating (can be useful for agitation)

**Cons**:
- **HIGH anticholinergic** effects
- **Orthostatic hypotension** (alpha-1 blockade)
- **Photosensitivity**
- **Lower seizure threshold**
- EPS (less than high-potency but still present)

**Dosing**: 200-800 mg/day

#### Thioridazine (Mellaril)

**Cons**:
- **Retinitis pigmentosa** (retinal deposits) at doses >800 mg/day
- **QTc prolongation** (worse than others)
- Rarely used now

**Dosing**: Max 800 mg/day (due to retinal toxicity)

### Rule of Thumb: High vs. Low Potency

| Feature | High Potency (Haloperidol) | Low Potency (Chlorpromazine) |
|---------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| **EPS** | +++ (very high) | + (lower) |
| **Sedation** | + (low) | +++ (high) |
| **Anticholinergic** | + (low) | +++ (high) |
| **Hypotension** | + (low) | +++ (high) |

**Mnemonic**: "**High potency, High EPS**" and "**Low potency, Low on your feet (hypotension)**"

## 🏥 Second-Generation (Atypical) Antipsychotics

### Risperidone (Risperdal)

**Mechanism**: D2 and 5-HT2A antagonist

**Characteristics**:
- One of the most commonly prescribed atypicals
- Available PO, orally disintegrating tablet, long-acting injectable (Risperdal Consta)

**Pros**:
- Effective for positive and negative symptoms
- Less sedation than olanzapine/quetiapine
- Available as LAI (long-acting injection)

**Cons**:
- **EPS at higher doses** (>6 mg) - most EPS of atypicals
- **Hyperprolactinemia** (highest of atypicals)
- Weight gain (moderate)
- Orthostatic hypotension

**Dosing**: 2-6 mg/day (higher doses increase EPS risk)

**Indications**:
- Schizophrenia
- Bipolar mania
- Autism irritability (FDA approved)
- Psychotic depression

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ Acts more like a typical antipsychotic at higher doses (>6 mg) due to EPS

---

### Olanzapine (Zyprexa)

**Mechanism**: D2, 5-HT2A, H1, M1 antagonist

**Pros**:
- **Very effective** for acute mania
- Available IM for acute agitation
- Available as LAI (Zyprexa Relprevv)

**Cons**:
- **WORST for weight gain** and metabolic syndrome
- **Worst for diabetes risk**
- Sedation
- Anticholinergic effects

**Dosing**: 10-20 mg/day

**Indications**:
- Schizophrenia
- **Bipolar mania** (very effective)
- Acute agitation (IM)
- Treatment-resistant depression (with fluoxetine = Symbyax)

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ **Most effective for acute mania but worst metabolic profile** - weigh benefits vs. risks

---

### Quetiapine (Seroquel)

**Mechanism**: D2, 5-HT2A, H1 antagonist

**Pros**:
- **Approved for bipolar depression** (one of few)
- Sedating (useful for sleep)
- Lower EPS risk
- Safe in Parkinson's disease

**Cons**:
- **Sedation** (significant)
- Weight gain (moderate-high)
- Metabolic syndrome
- **Cataracts** (baseline eye exam recommended)
- Orthostatic hypotension

**Dosing**:
- Schizophrenia: 300-800 mg/day
- Bipolar depression: 300 mg/day
- Sleep (off-label): 25-100 mg (NOT recommended - metabolic risks)

**Indications**:
- Schizophrenia
- **Bipolar mania and depression**
- **Major depression** (adjunct, brand name Seroquel XR)
- Psychosis in Parkinson's (low dose)

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ Good for bipolar depression; often misused for insomnia (avoid due to metabolic risks)

---

### Aripiprazole (Abilify)

**Mechanism**: **Partial D2 agonist** (unique!) + 5-HT2A antagonist

**Pros**:
- **Least weight gain** and metabolic effects
- Less sedation
- Lower EPS than risperidone
- Available LAI (Abilify Maintena, Aristada)

**Cons**:
- **Akathisia** (restlessness) - most common side effect
- Can be activating (insomnia)
- Takes longer to work than full antagonists

**Dosing**: 10-30 mg/day

**Indications**:
- Schizophrenia
- Bipolar mania
- **Depression adjunct** (when antidepressant not fully effective)
- Autism irritability

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ **Best metabolic profile** of atypicals; **akathisia** is main issue

---

### Ziprasidone (Geodon)

**Mechanism**: D2, 5-HT2A antagonist

**Pros**:
- **No weight gain** (weight neutral)
- Good metabolic profile
- Available IM

**Cons**:
- **QTc prolongation** (most of atypicals - get baseline ECG)
- **Must take with food** (absorption)
- BID dosing (adherence issue)
- Less effective than olanzapine/risperidone

**Dosing**: 80-160 mg/day (with food, divided BID)

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ Good if weight concern, but **check ECG** (QTc) and **must take with food**

---

### Lurasidone (Latuda)

**Mechanism**: D2, 5-HT2A antagonist

**Pros**:
- **Approved for bipolar depression**
- Weight neutral
- Good metabolic profile
- May help with cognitive symptoms

**Cons**:
- **Must take with food** (≥350 calories)
- Akathisia
- Nausea

**Dosing**: 40-120 mg/day (with ≥350 calorie meal)

**Indications**:
- Schizophrenia
- **Bipolar depression**

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ **Bipolar depression** option; must take with substantial meal

---

### Clozapine (Clozaril) ⭐ SPECIAL

**Mechanism**: D2, D4, 5-HT2A, H1, M1, α1 antagonist ("dirtiest" atypical)

**Indications**:
- **Treatment-resistant schizophrenia** (failed ≥2 antipsychotics)
- **Suicidality in schizophrenia** (only antipsychotic shown to reduce suicide)
- Psychosis in Parkinson's disease

**Pros**:
- **MOST EFFECTIVE antipsychotic** for treatment-resistant schizophrenia
- **Reduces suicide** (only one proven)
- Minimal EPS
- No tardive dyskinesia

**Cons**:
- **Agranulocytosis** (0.8%) - potentially fatal
- **Seizures** (dose-dependent, 5%)
- **Myocarditis** (rare but serious)
- Severe weight gain and metabolic syndrome
- Hypersalivation (sialorrhea)
- Sedation
- Constipation (can lead to ileus)

**Monitoring Required** (due to agranulocytosis risk):
- **Weekly CBC for 6 months**
- Then biweekly for 6 months
- Then monthly indefinitely
- **ANC (absolute neutrophil count) must be ≥1500** to continue
- Registry required (REMS program)

**Dosing**: Start 12.5 mg, titrate slowly to 300-450 mg/day

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ **Most effective but most dangerous** - reserved for treatment-resistant cases due to agranulocytosis risk

## ⚠️ Side Effects of Antipsychotics

### Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS)

#### 1. Acute Dystonia
**Timing**: Hours to days after starting

**Features**:
- Sustained muscle contractions
- **Torticollis** (neck twisting)
- **Oculogyric crisis** (eyes roll back)
- **Laryngospasm** (can be life-threatening)
- More common in young males

**Treatment**:
- **Benztropine** 1-2 mg IM/IV (anticholinergic)
- **Diphenhydramine** 25-50 mg IM/IV

#### 2. Akathisia
**Timing**: Days to weeks

**Features**:
- **Subjective restlessness**
- Can't sit still, pacing, fidgeting
- Very distressing to patient
- Can be mistaken for anxiety or agitation

**Treatment**:
- **Lower dose** of antipsychotic
- **Beta-blockers** (propranolol 30-90 mg/day)
- Benzodiazepines
- Anticholinergics (less effective)

#### 3. Parkinsonism
**Timing**: Weeks to months

**Features**:
- **Bradykinesia** (slowed movements)
- **Rigidity** (lead-pipe or cogwheel)
- **Resting tremor**
- **Masked facies**
- **Shuffling gait**

**Treatment**:
- **Lower dose** or switch to atypical
- **Benztropine** 1-2 mg BID
- **Amantadine** 100-300 mg/day

#### 4. Tardive Dyskinesia (TD)
**Timing**: Months to years (tardive = late)

**Features**:
- **Involuntary, repetitive movements**
- **Orofacial**: Tongue protrusion, lip smacking, chewing
- **Choreoathetoid movements**: Fingers, toes, trunk
- **May be irreversible**

**Risk factors**:
- Duration of treatment (cumulative)
- Older age
- Female
- Diabetes
- Higher doses
- Typicals > atypicals

**Treatment**:
- **Prevention is key** (use lowest effective dose, regular monitoring)
- **Stop or switch antipsychotic** if possible
- **Valbenazine** (Ingrezza) or **Deutetrabenazine** (Austedo) - FDA approved for TD
- Often **irreversible** even after stopping medication

**Monitoring**: Use **AIMS** (Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale) regularly

#### EPS Summary Table

| EPS Type | Timing | Key Features | Treatment |
|----------|--------|--------------|-----------|
| **Dystonia** | Hours-days | Muscle spasms, oculogyric crisis | Benztropine, Diphenhydramine |
| **Akathisia** | Days-weeks | Restlessness, pacing | Beta-blocker, lower dose |
| **Parkinsonism** | Weeks-months | Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia | Benztropine, Amantadine |
| **Tardive Dyskinesia** | Months-years | Orofacial movements, irreversible | Valbenazine, stop medication |

**Mnemonic for timing**: **"Dystonias develop Dynamically (fast), Akathisia is Aggravating (days), Parkinsonism is Progressive (weeks), Tardive is Tardy (late)"**

### Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)

**Definition**: Life-threatening reaction to antipsychotics

**Features** (Mnemonic: **FEVER**):
- **F**ever (high, >38-40°C)
- **E**ncephalopathy (altered mental status, confusion)
- **V**ital sign instability (tachycardia, labile BP)
- **E**levated enzymes (CK markedly elevated, >1000)
- **R**igidity (lead-pipe rigidity)

Also: Diaphoresis, tremor, leukocytosis, myoglobinuria

**Risk factors**:
- High-potency typicals (haloperidol)
- Rapid dose escalation
- IM administration
- Dehydration
- Agitation

**Treatment** (MEDICAL EMERGENCY):
1. **Stop antipsychotic immediately**
2. **Supportive care**: IV fluids, cooling, monitor renal function
3. **Dantrolene** (muscle relaxant) or **Bromocriptine** (dopamine agonist)
4. **Benzodiazepines** for agitation

**Prognosis**: 10-20% mortality if untreated; rhabdomyolysis → renal failure

**Clinical Pearl**: ✓ Looks similar to **serotonin syndrome** but NMS has **lead-pipe rigidity** (vs. hyperreflexia in serotonin syndrome), **slower onset** (days vs. hours)

---

### Metabolic Syndrome

**Components**:
- Weight gain
- Hyperglycemia/diabetes
- Dyslipidemia
- Hypertension

**Risk by medication** (Worst to Best):
1. **Clozapine** (worst)
2. **Olanzapine** (second worst)
3. Quetiapine
4. Risperidone
5. Paliperidone
6. **Aripiprazole** (best)
7. **Ziprasidone** (best)
8. **Lurasidone** (best)

**Mnemonic**: "**Clozapine and Olanzapine make you GAIN**"

**Monitoring**:
- **Baseline**: Weight, BMI, waist circumference, FBG, HbA1c, lipid panel
- **Regular**: Weight monthly × 3 months, then quarterly; labs at 3 months, then yearly

---

### Hyperprolactinemia

**Mechanism**: D2 blockade in tuberoinfundibular pathway → increased prolactin

**Symptoms**:
- **Women**: Galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction
- **Men**: Gynecomastia, erectile dysfunction, decreased libido
- **Both**: Decreased bone density (long-term)

**Risk by medication**:
1. **Risperidone** (highest)
2. **Paliperidone** (active metabolite of risperidone)
3. Typical antipsychotics
4. Quetiapine, Olanzapine (moderate)
5. **Aripiprazole, Clozapine** (lowest - may actually decrease prolactin)

**Management**:
- Switch to prolactin-sparing antipsychotic (aripiprazole, quetiapine, clozapine)
- If must continue: Consider dopamine agonist (cabergoline) - but can worsen psychosis

---

### QTc Prolongation

**Risk by medication** (Highest to Lowest):
1. **Thioridazine** (typical) - highest
2. **Ziprasidone**
3. **IV Haloperidol**
4. Quetiapine
5. Risperidone, Olanzapine (lower risk)

**Monitoring**:
- **Baseline ECG** if: Age >40, cardiac history, family history of sudden death, taking other QTc-prolonging drugs
- **Repeat ECG** if adding other QTc drugs or dose increases

**Management**:
- Correct electrolyte abnormalities (K+, Mg2+)
- Avoid other QTc-prolonging medications
- If QTc >500 ms, strongly consider switching medication

---

### Other Important Side Effects

#### Anticholinergic Effects
(More with low-potency typicals and clozapine)

**Mnemonic**: "**Blind as a bat, Mad as a hatter, Red as a beet, Hot as a hare, Dry as a bone**"
- Blurred vision, mydriasis
- Dry mouth
- Constipation (can → ileus with clozapine)
- Urinary retention
- Confusion (especially elderly)
- Tachycardia

#### Sedation
**Highest**: Clozapine, Olanzapine, Quetiapine, Chlorpromazine
**Lowest**: Aripiprazole, Haloperidol, Ziprasidone

#### Orthostatic Hypotension
**Mechanism**: Alpha-1 adrenergic blockade
**Highest risk**: Low-potency typicals, clozapine, quetiapine
**Management**: Slow titration, warn patient (fall risk)

#### Seizures
**Highest risk**: Clozapine (5%, dose-dependent)
**Management**: Consider prophylactic anticonvulsant if dose >600 mg/day

## 🎯 Indications Summary

### Schizophrenia
- **First-line**: Any second-generation (atypical)
- **Treatment-resistant**: **Clozapine** (after failing ≥2 antipsychotics)
- **Acute agitation**: IM Haloperidol, IM Olanzapine, IM Ziprasidone

### Bipolar Disorder
- **Acute mania**: Olanzapine, Risperidone, Quetiapine, Aripiprazole, or Haloperidol
- **Bipolar depression**: **Quetiapine**, **Lurasidone**, (Olanzapine + fluoxetine)
- **Maintenance**: Aripiprazole, Olanzapine, Quetiapine, Risperidone

### Other
- **Psychotic depression**: Antipsychotic + antidepressant
- **Delirium**: Low-dose haloperidol (controversial)
- **Parkinson's psychosis**: Quetiapine, Pimavanserin, Clozapine
- **Dementia agitation**: Avoid if possible (black box warning); Risperidone if necessary
- **Tourette's**: Haloperidol, Pimozide
- **Autism irritability**: Risperidone, Aripiprazole

## 📚 Must-Know for Shelf Exam

✓ **Typical vs. Atypical**: Typicals = more EPS; Atypicals = more metabolic
✓ **High-potency typicals** (Haloperidol) = high EPS, low sedation/anticholinergic
✓ **Low-potency typicals** (Chlorpromazine) = low EPS, high sedation/anticholinergic/hypotension
✓ **EPS timeline**: Dystonia (hours-days), Akathisia (days-weeks), Parkinsonism (weeks-months), TD (months-years)
✓ **Dystonia treatment**: Benztropine or Diphenhydramine IM
✓ **Akathisia treatment**: Beta-blockers (propranolol)
✓ **Tardive dyskinesia**: May be irreversible; use AIMS to monitor
✓ **NMS**: FEVER (Fever, Encephalopathy, Vital signs, Elevated CK, Rigidity)
✓ **Clozapine**: Most effective; agranulocytosis (needs weekly CBC monitoring initially)
✓ **Metabolic worst**: Olanzapine, Clozapine
✓ **Metabolic best**: Aripiprazole, Ziprasidone, Lurasidone
✓ **Hyperprolactinemia worst**: Risperidone
✓ **QTc worst**: Thioridazine, Ziprasidone
✓ **Bipolar depression**: Quetiapine, Lurasidone
✓ **Black box warning**: Increased mortality in elderly with dementia
