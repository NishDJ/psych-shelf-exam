# Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)

## 📋 DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria

### Core Requirements
**A.** Five (or more) of the following symptoms during the same 2-week period, representing a change from previous functioning. **At least one symptom must be either (1) or (2)**:

1. **Depressed mood** most of the day, nearly every day (subjective or observed)
2. **Markedly diminished interest or pleasure** in all/almost all activities (anhedonia)
3. Significant **weight loss** (>5% in a month) or **weight gain**, or decrease/increase in appetite
4. **Insomnia or hypersomnia** nearly every day
5. **Psychomotor agitation or retardation** (observable by others)
6. **Fatigue or loss of energy** nearly every day
7. Feelings of **worthlessness or excessive/inappropriate guilt**
8. Diminished **ability to think or concentrate**, or indecisiveness
9. Recurrent thoughts of **death**, suicidal ideation, suicide attempt, or specific plan

**B.** Symptoms cause clinically significant distress or impairment in functioning

**C.** Not attributable to substance or medical condition

**D.** Not better explained by schizophrenia spectrum or other psychotic disorder

**E.** Never had a manic or hypomanic episode (would be bipolar disorder)

### 🎯 Mnemonic: **SIG E CAPS**
- **S**leep disturbance
- **I**nterest loss (anhedonia)
- **G**uilt/worthlessness
- **E**nergy loss
- **C**oncentration problems
- **A**ppetite/weight changes
- **P**sychomotor changes
- **S**uicidal ideation

## 📊 Epidemiology

- **Lifetime prevalence**: 15-20%
- **Female:Male ratio**: 2:1
- **Peak onset**: 20s-30s, but can occur at any age
- **Recurrence**: ~50% after first episode, ~80% after two episodes
- **Average episode duration**: 6-12 months if untreated

## 🎭 Clinical Subtypes & Specifiers

### Severity Specifiers
- **Mild**: Few symptoms beyond minimum required; mild impairment
- **Moderate**: Symptoms/impairment between mild and severe
- **Severe**: Most symptoms present; marked impairment
- **With psychotic features**: Delusions or hallucinations

### Important Specifiers

#### Melancholic Features
- Loss of pleasure in ALL activities
- Lack of reactivity to pleasurable stimuli
- PLUS three or more:
  - Distinct quality of depressed mood (different from grief)
  - Depression worse in the morning
  - Early morning awakening (≥2 hours before usual)
  - Marked psychomotor retardation or agitation
  - Significant anorexia or weight loss
  - Excessive or inappropriate guilt

#### Atypical Features
- Mood reactivity (brightens in response to positive events)
- PLUS two or more:
  - Significant weight gain or increased appetite
  - Hypersomnia
  - Leaden paralysis (heavy feelings in arms/legs)
  - Rejection sensitivity (long-standing pattern)
- **Treatment note**: MAOIs particularly effective (but SSRIs still first-line)

#### Psychotic Features
- **Mood-congruent**: Delusions/hallucinations consistent with depression (guilt, punishment, worthlessness, disease)
- **Mood-incongruent**: Content not consistent with depressive themes
- **Treatment**: Antidepressant + antipsychotic (or ECT)

#### Peripartum Onset
- Onset during pregnancy or within 4 weeks postpartum
- **Postpartum blues**: 50-80% of mothers, resolves in 2 weeks, supportive care only
- **Postpartum depression**: 10-15% of mothers, requires treatment
- **Postpartum psychosis**: 0.1-0.2%, psychiatric emergency, risk of infanticide

#### Seasonal Pattern
- Regular temporal relationship between mood episodes and time of year
- Typically depressive episodes in fall/winter, remission in spring/summer
- **Treatment**: Light therapy (10,000 lux, 30 min daily), antidepressants

#### Anxious Distress
- Feeling keyed up or tense
- Restlessness
- Difficulty concentrating due to worry
- Fear that something awful may happen
- Feeling of losing control

## 🔬 Etiology & Pathophysiology

### Biological Factors
- **Monoamine hypothesis**: Decreased serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine
- **HPA axis dysfunction**: Elevated cortisol, abnormal dexamethasone suppression test
- **Neuroplasticity**: Decreased hippocampal volume, reduced BDNF
- **Genetic**: 40-50% heritability; higher if early onset

### Psychosocial Factors
- **Stressful life events**: Especially losses, relationship problems
- **Childhood trauma**: Abuse, neglect
- **Lack of social support**
- **Cognitive patterns**: Negative automatic thoughts, learned helplessness

### Medical Causes to Rule Out
- **Endocrine**: Hypothyroidism, Cushing's, Addison's
- **Neurological**: Stroke, Parkinson's, MS, dementia
- **Nutritional**: B12, folate, vitamin D deficiency
- **Infectious**: HIV, syphilis, Lyme disease
- **Medications**: Beta-blockers, steroids, interferon, isotretinoin
- **Substances**: Alcohol, sedatives, withdrawal states

## 💊 Treatment

### Acute Phase (6-12 weeks)

#### Mild to Moderate Depression
**First-line options** (equally effective):
- **Psychotherapy alone**: CBT, IPT, behavioral activation
- **Antidepressant medication**: SSRI (most common first choice)
- **Combination**: Slightly more effective than either alone

#### Moderate to Severe Depression
**First-line**:
- **SSRI or SNRI** + consider psychotherapy
- Examples: Sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, citalopram

**Second-line** (if SSRIs fail or not tolerated):
- Different SSRI/SNRI
- Bupropion (good if fatigue, low energy, or concern for sexual side effects)
- Mirtazapine (good if insomnia, poor appetite)

**Adjunctive treatments**:
- Exercise (evidence-based)
- Sleep hygiene
- Psychotherapy (CBT most evidence)

#### Severe or Psychotic Depression
- **Antidepressant + antipsychotic**
- **ECT** - most effective acute treatment (70-90% response rate)
- Hospitalization if suicidal or severely impaired

### Continuation Phase (4-9 months after remission)
- **Continue same medication at same dose**
- Prevent relapse (50% risk if stopped early)

### Maintenance Phase (Long-term)
**Indications for maintenance**:
- ≥3 episodes of MDD
- 2 episodes + risk factors (family history, early onset, severe episodes)
- **Duration**: Often indefinite

### Medication Specifics

#### SSRIs (First-line)
| Medication | Starting Dose | Therapeutic Dose | Notes |
|------------|--------------|------------------|-------|
| Sertraline | 50 mg | 50-200 mg | GI side effects common initially |
| Escitalopram | 10 mg | 10-20 mg | Fewest drug interactions |
| Fluoxetine | 20 mg | 20-80 mg | Long half-life (good for adherence) |
| Citalopram | 20 mg | 20-40 mg | Max 40 mg (QTc prolongation risk) |
| Paroxetine | 20 mg | 20-50 mg | Most anticholinergic, worst withdrawal |

**Common side effects**: GI upset, sexual dysfunction (60-70%), headache, insomnia/sedation, weight gain (long-term)

**Timeline**: 2-4 weeks for initial effect, 6-8 weeks for full effect

**Black box warning**: Increased suicidal ideation in patients <25 years (monitor closely first 4 weeks)

#### SNRIs
- **Venlafaxine**: 75-225 mg; can increase BP at higher doses
- **Duloxetine**: 30-60 mg; also approved for chronic pain
- **Desvenlafaxine**: 50 mg; active metabolite of venlafaxine

#### Other Antidepressants
- **Bupropion**: 150-450 mg; dopamine/NE; no sexual side effects; avoid if seizure risk
- **Mirtazapine**: 15-45 mg; sedating, increases appetite; good for insomnia/weight loss
- **Trazodone**: 150-400 mg (for depression); often used 50-100 mg for insomnia
- **Vilazodone, vortioxetine**: Newer agents; less sexual dysfunction

#### MAOIs (Rarely used now)
- **Phenelzine, tranylcypromine**
- Effective for atypical depression
- Requires tyramine-free diet (risk of hypertensive crisis)
- Many drug interactions

### Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT)
**Indications**:
- Severe depression not responsive to medications
- Psychotic depression
- Catatonia
- Need for rapid response (severe suicidality, not eating)
- Patient preference
- Pregnancy (safest option for severe depression)

**Efficacy**: 70-90% response rate (higher than medications)

**Side effects**: Short-term memory impairment (usually temporary), headache, muscle aches

**Procedure**: 6-12 treatments over 2-4 weeks

### Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)
- Non-invasive brain stimulation
- For treatment-resistant depression
- Fewer side effects than ECT (no anesthesia, no memory problems)
- Daily treatments for 4-6 weeks

### Psychotherapy

#### Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
- **Duration**: 12-16 sessions typically
- **Focus**: Identify and modify negative thought patterns
- **Technique**: Cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation
- **Evidence**: Strong evidence, as effective as medications for mild-moderate MDD

#### Interpersonal Therapy (IPT)
- **Focus**: Relationship issues, role transitions, grief
- **Duration**: 12-16 sessions
- **Evidence**: Effective for depression, especially with interpersonal triggers

#### Behavioral Activation
- **Focus**: Increase engagement in rewarding activities
- **Simpler than full CBT**
- **Effective** for depression

## 🚨 Suicide Risk Assessment

### Risk Factors
**High-risk features**:
- Previous suicide attempt (strongest predictor)
- Current suicidal ideation with plan and intent
- Access to lethal means
- Recent psychiatric hospitalization (especially first week after discharge)
- Male gender (higher completion rate)
- Age >45 or adolescent
- Social isolation
- Comorbid substance use
- Psychosis
- Recent significant loss
- Chronic medical illness

### Protective Factors
- Strong social support
- Religious beliefs
- Responsibility to children/family
- Positive therapeutic relationship
- Access to care
- Problem-solving skills

### Assessment
Ask directly:
1. "Are you having thoughts of hurting yourself?"
2. "Have you thought about how you would do it?" (Plan)
3. "Do you intend to act on these thoughts?" (Intent)
4. "Do you have access to [means mentioned]?"
5. "What's stopping you?" (Protective factors)

### Management
- **Imminent risk**: Hospitalization (voluntary or involuntary)
- **Moderate risk**: Increase monitoring, remove means, safety planning, consider hospitalization
- **Low risk**: Outpatient treatment, safety planning, follow-up

## 🔄 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Key Distinguishing Features |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Bipolar Depression** | History of mania/hypomania; may have psychotic features, atypical features |
| **Persistent Depressive Disorder** | Chronic course (≥2 years); may be less severe |
| **Adjustment Disorder** | Clear stressor; <3 months after stressor; resolves within 6 months |
| **Grief/Bereavement** | Following loss; waves of sadness; self-esteem intact; not pervasive |
| **Hypothyroidism** | Fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance; check TSH |
| **Substance Use** | Temporal relationship with use/withdrawal; improves with abstinence |
| **Dysthymia** | Chronic (≥2 years) but less severe than MDD |

## 📈 Prognosis

- **Response to treatment**: 60-70% with first antidepressant
- **Remission**: May take trying 2-3 medications
- **Recurrence**: High (50-80% depending on number of episodes)
- **Chronic course**: 10-20% become chronic without full recovery
- **Functional impairment**: Often persists even after symptom resolution

## 🎓 Clinical Pearls

1. **Always screen for bipolar** before starting antidepressants (ask about manic/hypomanic episodes)
2. **Suicide risk may increase initially** when starting antidepressants (increased energy before mood improves)
3. **Sexual side effects** are major cause of non-adherence (discuss openly)
4. **Medication trial = adequate dose for adequate duration** (6-8 weeks at therapeutic dose)
5. **Discontinuation syndrome** can occur with abrupt cessation (especially paroxetine, venlafaxine)
6. **Psychotic depression will not respond to antidepressant alone** - needs antipsychotic or ECT
7. **Atypical features** respond well to MAOIs (but SSRIs still first-line due to safety)
8. **ECT is most effective treatment** - don't wait too long in treatment-resistant cases
9. **Maintenance treatment** prevents recurrence - don't stop too soon
10. **Combination therapy** (medication + psychotherapy) often better than either alone

## ❓ Common Exam Scenarios

**Scenario 1**: Patient with fatigue, poor sleep, anhedonia × 3 weeks
- **Answer**: MDD; start SSRI + psychotherapy

**Scenario 2**: Depressed patient improves on SSRI but has sexual dysfunction
- **Answer**: Switch to bupropion or add bupropion; or try mirtazapine

**Scenario 3**: Severe depression, won't eat/drink, high suicide risk
- **Answer**: Consider ECT (fastest, most effective); may need hospitalization

**Scenario 4**: Depression in pregnancy
- **Answer**: Mild: psychotherapy; Severe: SSRI (sertraline preferred) or ECT; weigh risks/benefits

**Scenario 5**: Patient better after 6 weeks on SSRI, wants to stop
- **Answer**: Continue for 4-9 months minimum (continuation phase) to prevent relapse
