# Bipolar Disorder

## 🎯 Overview

Bipolar disorder is characterized by episodes of mood disturbance including mania, hypomania, and depression. The key distinction from Major Depressive Disorder is the presence of manic or hypomanic episodes.

## 📋 Types of Bipolar Disorder

### Bipolar I Disorder
- **Defining feature**: ≥1 manic episode (may or may not have depressive episodes)
- More severe form
- Lifetime prevalence: ~1%
- Equal in males and females

### Bipolar II Disorder
- **Defining feature**: ≥1 hypomanic episode AND ≥1 major depressive episode
- No manic episodes (would be Bipolar I)
- Lifetime prevalence: 0.5-1%
- More common in females

### Cyclothymic Disorder
- Chronic fluctuating mood (≥2 years in adults, ≥1 year in children/adolescents)
- Numerous periods of hypomanic and depressive symptoms
- Symptoms don't meet full criteria for hypomanic or depressive episode
- Never without symptoms for >2 months

## 📋 DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria

### Manic Episode

**A.** Abnormally and persistently elevated, expansive, or irritable mood AND increased goal-directed activity or energy for **≥1 week** (or any duration if hospitalization required)

**B.** During the mood disturbance, **≥3** of the following (4 if mood is only irritable):

1. Inflated self-esteem or **grandiosity**
2. Decreased need for **sleep** (feels rested after 3 hours)
3. More **talkative** than usual or pressured speech
4. **Flight of ideas** or racing thoughts
5. **Distractibility**
6. Increase in goal-directed **activity** or psychomotor agitation
7. Excessive involvement in activities with high potential for painful consequences (**risky behavior**: sexual indiscretions, foolish investments, spending sprees)

**C.** Mood disturbance causes marked impairment OR hospitalization required OR psychotic features

**D.** Not due to substances or medical condition

### 🎯 Mnemonic: **DIG FAST**
- **D**istractibility
- **I**rresponsibility (risky behavior)
- **G**randiosity
- **F**light of ideas
- **A**ctivity increase
- **S**leep deficit (decreased need)
- **T**alkativeness

### Hypomanic Episode

**Same criteria as manic episode EXCEPT**:
- Duration: **≥4 consecutive days** (instead of 7)
- **Not severe enough** to cause marked impairment or hospitalization
- **No psychotic features**
- Episode represents clear change from usual functioning
- Observable by others

### Major Depressive Episode
(See Major Depressive Disorder file for full criteria)
- Same criteria as MDD
- ≥2 weeks of depressed mood or anhedonia + other symptoms

## 📊 Epidemiology

| Feature | Bipolar I | Bipolar II |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Lifetime prevalence | ~1% | 0.5-1% |
| Gender ratio | 1:1 | Slight F>M |
| Age of onset | Late teens-20s | Slightly later than BP I |
| Genetic component | 80-90% heritability | High heritability |

**Key facts**:
- 10-15% of adolescents with MDD will develop bipolar disorder
- Higher rates in first-degree relatives (10-fold increase)
- Earlier onset = worse prognosis
- Patients spend more time depressed than manic (~3:1 ratio)

## 🎭 Clinical Presentations

### Manic Episode - Classic Presentation
"28-year-old brought in by police for disruptive behavior. Patient has not slept in 3 days, spent $10,000 on credit cards, very talkative, difficult to interrupt, says he's developing a cure for cancer despite no medical training. Jumped from topic to topic during interview."

### Hypomanic Episode - Classic Presentation
"25-year-old woman presents for decreased need for sleep × 5 days, feels great, very productive at work, started multiple new projects, more talkative than usual. Coworkers noticed she's 'different' but she's still functioning well at her job."

### Mixed Features
- Simultaneously meet criteria for both manic/hypomanic AND depressive episode
- Or predominant mood episode with ≥3 symptoms of opposite pole
- Higher suicide risk than pure mania or depression

### Rapid Cycling
- **Definition**: ≥4 mood episodes in 12 months
- Worse prognosis
- More common in women
- Associated with hypothyroidism, antidepressant use

## 🔬 Etiology

### Biological
- **Genetic**: 80-90% heritability; strongest genetic component of major psychiatric disorders
- **Neurotransmitters**: Dysregulation of dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine
- **Neuroanatomy**: Prefrontal cortex and amygdala abnormalities
- **Circadian rhythm**: Disruption common; sleep deprivation can trigger mania

### Environmental Triggers
- **Stressful life events**: Can trigger episodes
- **Sleep deprivation**: Common trigger for mania
- **Substance use**: Especially stimulants
- **Antidepressants**: Can trigger mania (why mood stabilizer needed)
- **Seasonal changes**: Some patients have seasonal pattern

### Medical/Substance Causes to Rule Out
- **Hyperthyroidism**: Can mimic mania
- **Stimulants**: Cocaine, amphetamines, caffeine (excessive)
- **Steroids**: Can cause manic symptoms
- **CNS disorders**: Brain tumors, MS, stroke, seizures
- **Medications**: Corticosteroids, levodopa, antidepressants

## 💊 Treatment

### Acute Mania

#### First-line Monotherapy
1. **Lithium** - Gold standard; most evidence
2. **Valproate** (divalproex)
3. **Atypical antipsychotics**:
   - Olanzapine
   - Risperidone
   - Quetiapine
   - Aripiprazole

#### Severe Mania
- **Combination therapy**: Mood stabilizer + antipsychotic
- **Hospitalization** often required
- **Benzodiazepines**: For acute agitation (lorazepam, clonazepam)

### Acute Bipolar Depression

#### First-line
- **Quetiapine** (best evidence for bipolar depression)
- **Lurasidone**
- **Lamotrigine** (more for maintenance/prevention)
- **Lithium or valproate** (if already on for maintenance)

#### Second-line
- **Antidepressant + mood stabilizer** (NEVER antidepressant alone!)
  - Use with caution; may trigger mania
  - SSRI or bupropion preferred (lower risk of mood switching)
  - Always combined with mood stabilizer

### Maintenance Treatment (Prevent Recurrence)

#### First-line
- **Lithium** - Best evidence for relapse prevention and suicide reduction
- **Valproate**
- **Lamotrigine** - Better for preventing depression than mania
- **Atypical antipsychotics**: Quetiapine, aripiprazole, olanzapine

#### Key Principles
- Lifelong treatment usually necessary
- Don't stop medications when feeling better
- Mood stabilizer always needed (backbone of treatment)
- Psychotherapy (e.g., psychoeducation, CBT, family therapy) important adjunct

### Medication Details

#### Lithium
**Dosing**: Target level 0.6-1.2 mEq/L (acute mania); 0.6-0.8 (maintenance)

**Monitoring**:
- **Before starting**: TSH, Cr, BUN, pregnancy test, ECG (if >40 or cardiac history)
- **Regular**: Lithium level (q3-6 months), TSH, Cr/BUN (q6-12 months)
- **Check level**: 12 hours post-dose

**Side effects**:
- GI upset, tremor, polyuria/polydipsia, weight gain, acne
- Hypothyroidism (5-35%)
- Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus
- Cognitive dulling

**Toxicity** (>1.5 mEq/L):
- Coarse tremor, confusion, ataxia, dysarthria
- Severe (>2.5): Seizures, coma, death
- Risk increased by: Dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, thiazides

**Benefits**:
- ⭐ **Only medication proven to reduce suicide**
- Most evidence for long-term efficacy
- Treats and prevents both mania and depression

#### Valproate (Divalproex)
**Dosing**: 750-2000 mg/day; target level 50-125 μg/mL

**Monitoring**:
- LFTs, CBC (before and periodically)
- Consider level monitoring
- Pregnancy test (teratogenic)

**Side effects**:
- GI upset, tremor, weight gain, hair loss
- Thrombocytopenia, elevated LFTs
- Pancreatitis (rare but serious)
- PCOS in women

**Contraindications**:
- Pregnancy (neural tube defects, decreased IQ)
- Liver disease
- Urea cycle disorders

#### Lamotrigine
**Dosing**: Slow titration required (weeks); target 200 mg/day

**Titration important**: To prevent Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)
- **Slower if on valproate** (valproate increases lamotrigine levels)
- **Faster if on carbamazepine** (decreases lamotrigine levels)

**Side effects**:
- Generally well-tolerated
- Rash (10%; stop if rash develops)
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (<0.1% but serious)

**Benefits**:
- Better for preventing depression than mania
- Few side effects
- No weight gain

#### Atypical Antipsychotics

| Medication | Pros | Cons | Acute Mania | Acute Depression | Maintenance |
|------------|------|------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Quetiapine | Good for depression | Sedation, metabolic | ✓ | ✓✓✓ | ✓ |
| Olanzapine | Effective for mania | Weight gain++, metabolic | ✓✓✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Aripiprazole | Less metabolic | Akathisia | ✓✓ | - | ✓ |
| Risperidone | Effective | EPS, prolactin | ✓✓ | - | ✓ |
| Lurasidone | Good for depression | Take with food | - | ✓✓ | - |

**Metabolic monitoring**: Weight, glucose, lipids (baseline and periodically)

### Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT)
**Indications**:
- Severe mania not responding to medications
- Severe bipolar depression
- Psychotic features
- Pregnancy (safest option)
- Rapid response needed

**Efficacy**: Very effective for both mania and depression

### Psychotherapy
- **Psychoeducation**: Understanding illness, medications, triggers
- **CBT**: Identify triggers, early warning signs
- **Family-focused therapy**: Reduce expressed emotion, improve communication
- **Interpersonal and social rhythm therapy**: Regulate sleep-wake cycles, daily routines

## 🚨 Emergencies & Red Flags

### Psychiatric Emergencies
1. **Suicidal ideation**: Higher risk in mixed episodes and bipolar depression
2. **Homicidal ideation**: Can occur in paranoid/psychotic mania
3. **Reckless behavior**: Sexual promiscuity, spending, substance use
4. **Psychosis**: May require hospitalization
5. **Medication non-adherence**: Leading cause of relapse

### Medical Emergencies
1. **Lithium toxicity**: Tremor, confusion, ataxia → seizures, coma
2. **Serotonin syndrome**: If on antidepressant + other serotonergic agents
3. **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome**: Fever, rigidity, elevated CK (from antipsychotics)
4. **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome**: Rash from lamotrigine

## 🔄 Differential Diagnosis

| Condition | Key Distinguishing Features |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Major Depressive Disorder** | No history of mania/hypomania |
| **Substance-Induced** | Temporal relationship with substance use; resolves with abstinence |
| **ADHD** | Chronic since childhood; no distinct episodes; responds to stimulants |
| **Borderline Personality** | Mood shifts rapid (hours), reactive to interpersonal stress; chronic pattern |
| **Schizoaffective Disorder** | Psychosis present even without mood symptoms |
| **Cyclothymia** | Subsyndromal symptoms; chronic course; no full manic/major depressive episodes |
| **Hyperthyroidism** | Check TSH; weight loss, heat intolerance, tremor |

## 📈 Course & Prognosis

- **Chronic illness**: Requires lifelong treatment in most cases
- **Recurrence**: Very high without treatment (>90%)
- **Functional impairment**: Significant between episodes for many patients
- **Suicide risk**: 15-20% lifetime (highest of all psychiatric disorders)
- **Substance use**: 60% comorbidity with substance use disorders
- **Medication adherence**: Poor (30-50% non-adherent)

**Predictors of poor prognosis**:
- Early onset
- Rapid cycling
- Mixed features
- Psychotic features
- Substance use comorbidity
- Poor medication adherence

## 🎓 Clinical Pearls

1. **⚠️ NEVER give antidepressant alone in bipolar disorder** - Always with mood stabilizer (risk of inducing mania)
2. **Lithium is the only medication proven to reduce suicide** in bipolar disorder
3. **Patients spend 3× more time depressed than manic** - depression is the dominant pole
4. **Screen all depressed patients for history of mania/hypomania** before starting antidepressant
5. **Sleep deprivation** is a common trigger for manic episodes
6. **Medication non-adherence** is the #1 cause of relapse
7. **Mixed episodes have highest suicide risk** (more than pure mania or depression)
8. **Rapid cycling** = worse prognosis, harder to treat
9. **Pregnancy**: Lithium = Ebstein anomaly; Valproate = neural tube defects, decreased IQ (worst)
10. **Substance use very common** (60% comorbidity) - worsens outcome
11. **Bipolar II patients often misdiagnosed as MDD** - hypomanic episodes may not be reported
12. **Lamotrigine requires slow titration** to prevent Stevens-Johnson Syndrome
13. **Weight gain common** with mood stabilizers (olanzapine > valproate > lithium)
14. **Thyroid and renal monitoring** essential with lithium
15. **Family history very important** - strong genetic component

## ❓ Common Exam Scenarios

**Scenario 1**: Depressed patient started on SSRI, now presents with decreased sleep, racing thoughts, increased energy
- **Answer**: Antidepressant-induced mania; stop SSRI, start mood stabilizer

**Scenario 2**: Patient with 3 days of grandiosity, decreased sleep, spending sprees
- **Answer**: Not mania (need ≥7 days); could be hypomanic episode; need more history

**Scenario 3**: Young patient with recurrent depression; family history of bipolar disorder
- **Answer**: Screen for hypomania before starting antidepressant; may be Bipolar II

**Scenario 4**: Manic patient, lithium level 2.0, confused, tremor
- **Answer**: Lithium toxicity; stop lithium, check renal function, may need hemodialysis

**Scenario 5**: Pregnant woman with bipolar disorder wants to start medication
- **Answer**: Discuss risks/benefits; consider ECT or continuing current med; avoid valproate (most teratogenic)

**Scenario 6**: Patient on lithium for years, now has polyuria and polydipsia
- **Answer**: Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus; check renal function; may need to switch medication or add amiloride
