# Psychiatric Emergencies - Quick Reference

## 🚨 Life-Threatening Emergencies

### 1. Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)

**Cause**: Reaction to antipsychotics (especially high-potency typicals like haloperidol)

**Presentation - FEVER**:
- **F**ever (>38-40°C, high fever)
- **E**ncephalopathy (altered mental status, confusion)
- **V**ital sign instability (tachycardia, labile BP)
- **E**levated enzymes (**CK markedly elevated** >1000, often >10,000)
- **R**igidity (**lead-pipe rigidity**)

Also: Diaphoresis, tremor, leukocytosis, myoglobinuria (→ acute renal failure)

**Timeline**: Days after starting/increasing antipsychotic

**Treatment** (EMERGENCY):
1. **Stop antipsychotic immediately**
2. **Supportive care**: IV fluids, cooling blankets, ICU monitoring
3. **Dantrolene** (muscle relaxant) or **Bromocriptine** (dopamine agonist)
4. **Benzodiazepines** for agitation
5. Monitor renal function (rhabdomyolysis risk)

**Mortality**: 10-20% if untreated

**Restart antipsychotics**: Wait ≥2 weeks, use different class (atypical), low dose

---

### 2. Serotonin Syndrome

**Cause**: Excess serotonergic activity (SSRIs + MAOIs, multiple serotonergic drugs)

**Presentation - Classic Triad**:
1. **Autonomic instability**: Hyperthermia, tachycardia, diaphoresis, BP lability, mydriasis
2. **Neuromuscular hyperactivity**:
   - **Clonus** (spontaneous or inducible, especially lower extremities)
   - **Hyperreflexia** (more pronounced in lower extremities)
   - Tremor, rigidity, myoclonus
3. **Mental status changes**: Agitation, confusion, delirium

**Timeline**: **Hours** after medication change (rapid onset)

**Hunter Criteria** (most sensitive):
- Serotonergic agent + one of:
  - Spontaneous clonus
  - Inducible clonus + agitation or diaphoresis
  - Ocular clonus + agitation or diaphoresis
  - Tremor + hyperreflexia
  - Hypertonia + temp >38°C + ocular clonus or inducible clonus

**Treatment**:
1. **Stop serotonergic agents**
2. **Supportive care**: Cooling, fluids, BP management
3. **Benzodiazepines**: Agitation, seizures
4. **Cyproheptadine**: Serotonin antagonist (12 mg initially, then 2 mg q2hr; max 32 mg/day)
5. **Avoid restraints** (increase heat production)

**NMS vs. Serotonin Syndrome**:
| Feature | NMS | Serotonin Syndrome |
|---------|-----|-------------------|
| **Onset** | Days | Hours |
| **Rigidity** | Lead-pipe | Variable |
| **Reflexes** | Normal/decreased | **Hyperreflexia, clonus** |
| **Cause** | Antipsychotics | Serotonergic drugs |
| **CK** | Very high (>10,000) | Mildly elevated |

### 3. Lithium Toxicity

**Causes**:
- Overdose
- Dehydration
- Renal impairment
- Drug interactions: **NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, thiazides** (decrease lithium clearance)

**Presentation by Level**:

**Mild (1.5-2.0 mEq/L)**:
- GI: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
- Fine tremor
- Polyuria
- Muscle weakness

**Moderate (2.0-2.5 mEq/L)**:
- Confusion, lethargy
- **Coarse tremor**
- Ataxia
- Dysarthria, slurred speech
- Nystagmus
- Muscle fasciculations

**Severe (>2.5 mEq/L)**:
- Seizures
- Coma
- Cardiovascular collapse
- Death

**Treatment**:
1. **Stop lithium**
2. **Check lithium level, electrolytes, renal function**
3. **IV fluids** (normal saline)
4. **Hemodialysis** if:
   - Level >4.0 mEq/L
   - Severe symptoms (seizures, coma, arrhythmias)
   - Renal failure
   - Levels >2.5 mEq/L with declining renal function
5. **Whole bowel irrigation** (if acute overdose)

**Note**: Lithium not removed by activated charcoal

### 4. Anticholinergic Toxicity

**Causes**:
- TCAs (overdose)
- Low-potency antipsychotics
- Antihistamines
- Antiparkinsonian meds (benztropine)
- Multiple anticholinergic medications

**Presentation - Classic Mnemonics**:

"**Blind as a bat, Mad as a hatter, Red as a beet, Hot as a hare, Dry as a bone, The bowel and bladder lose their tone, The heart runs alone**"

**Central**:
- **Delirium, confusion**
- Hallucinations (typically visual, "mad as a hatter")
- Agitation
- Seizures (severe)

**Peripheral**:
- **Mydriasis** (dilated pupils, "blind as a bat")
- **Dry mucous membranes** ("dry as a bone")
- **Flushing** ("red as a beet")
- **Hyperthermia** ("hot as a hare")
- **Urinary retention**
- **Decreased bowel sounds**
- **Tachycardia** ("heart runs alone")

**Treatment**:
1. **Supportive care**: Cooling, fluids, monitoring
2. **Benzodiazepines**: Agitation, seizures
3. **Physostigmine**: Anticholinesterase (reversal agent)
   - **Indications**: Severe agitation, seizures, hemodynamic instability
   - **Contraindications**: Cardiac conduction abnormalities (can cause asystole)
   - Dose: 0.5-2 mg IV slowly
4. **Activated charcoal** (if recent ingestion)

---

### 5. TCA Overdose

**Highly Lethal** - Leading cause of death from medication overdose

**Presentation**:
- **Cardiac toxicity** (most dangerous):
  - **QRS widening** (>100 ms = concerning; >160 ms = severe)
  - Ventricular arrhythmias
  - Heart block
  - Hypotension
- **CNS**: Sedation, coma, seizures
- **Anticholinergic** symptoms (as above)

**Treatment** (ICU/CCU):
1. **Activated charcoal** (if <1 hour since ingestion)
2. **Sodium bicarbonate**:
   - **For QRS >100 ms** or arrhythmias
   - Bolus 1-2 mEq/kg, then infusion
   - Goal: Narrow QRS, alkalinize serum (pH 7.45-7.55)
3. **Benzodiazepines**: Seizures
4. **Avoid**:
   - Physostigmine (can worsen cardiac toxicity)
   - Class IA, IC antiarrhythmics (worsen conduction)
5. **Supportive care**: Fluids, pressors if needed, cardiac monitoring

**Never give TCAs to acutely suicidal patients** (lethal in overdose)

### 6. Catatonia

**Features** (need ≥3):
1. **Stupor** (minimal response to stimuli)
2. **Catalepsy** (passive induction of posture)
3. **Waxy flexibility** (slight resistance to positioning)
4. **Mutism**
5. **Negativism** (opposition to instructions)
6. **Posturing**
7. **Mannerisms**
8. **Stereotypy**
9. **Agitation** (not influenced by external stimuli)
10. **Grimacing**
11. **Echolalia** (repeating speech)
12. **Echopraxia** (mimicking movements)

**Causes**:
- **Schizophrenia** (historically most common association)
- **Mood disorders** (bipolar, MDD - actually most common cause)
- **Medical conditions**: Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis, neuroleptic malignant syndrome
- **Medications**: Antipsychotics

**Life-threatening complications**:
- **Malignant catatonia**: Fever, autonomic instability, rigidity
- Dehydration, malnutrition
- Aspiration
- Thromboembolism

**Diagnosis**:
- **Lorazepam challenge**: Give 1-2 mg lorazepam IV/IM, observe for improvement

**Treatment**:
1. **Benzodiazepines** (first-line):
   - **Lorazepam** 1-2 mg IV/IM, repeat q2-4hr
   - Can use high doses (up to 24 mg/day)
   - Response in 80% of cases
2. **ECT** (if benzodiazepines fail):
   - Most effective treatment
   - Response rate >80%
3. **Treat underlying cause**
4. **Supportive care**: Hydration, nutrition, DVT prophylaxis

**Avoid antipsychotics** (can worsen catatonia)

### 7. Delirium

**Presentation**:
- **Acute onset** (hours to days)
- **Fluctuating course**
- **Inattention** (key feature)
- **Disorganized thinking**
- **Altered level of consciousness**

**Hyperactive**: Agitation, hallucinations (more obvious)
**Hypoactive**: Lethargy, withdrawal (often missed)
**Mixed**: Both

**Causes** (Mnemonic: **I WATCH DEATH**):
- **I**nfection (UTI, pneumonia, sepsis)
- **W**ithdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines)
- **A**cute metabolic (electrolytes, glucose, liver, kidney)
- **T**rauma, pain
- **C**NS pathology (stroke, seizure, bleed)
- **H**ypoxia
- **D**eficiencies (B12, thiamine)
- **E**ndocrine (thyroid, cortisol)
- **A**cute vascular (MI, shock)
- **T**oxins, medications
- **H**eavy metals

**Workup**:
- Vital signs (including oxygen saturation)
- Labs: CBC, CMP, LFTs, TSH, B12, UA, toxicology
- Head CT (if neurological signs)
- EEG (if concern for seizure)
- LP (if concern for meningitis/encephalitis)

**Treatment**:
1. **Treat underlying cause** (most important!)
2. **Non-pharmacologic**:
   - Reorientation
   - Familiar objects
   - Normal sleep-wake cycle
   - Mobilize early
   - Minimize restraints
3. **Pharmacologic** (if needed for safety):
   - **Haloperidol** 0.5-1 mg PO/IM (traditional choice)
   - Atypical antipsychotics (quetiapine, risperidone) - preferred by some
   - **Avoid in**: Lewy body dementia, Parkinson's (use quetiapine)
   - **Avoid benzodiazepines** (except alcohol/benzo withdrawal)
4. **Avoid restraints** if possible (worsen agitation)

**Delirium vs. Dementia**:
| Feature | Delirium | Dementia |
|---------|----------|----------|
| **Onset** | Acute (hours-days) | Insidious (months-years) |
| **Course** | **Fluctuating** | Progressive, stable |
| **Attention** | **Impaired** (key!) | Intact (until late) |
| **Consciousness** | Altered | Clear |
| **Reversibility** | Often reversible | Usually irreversible |
| **Causes** | Medical illness | Neurodegenerative |

## 🚨 Acute Agitation Management

### De-escalation (First-line)
1. **Ensure safety**: Staff, other patients, exit route
2. **Verbal de-escalation**:
   - Calm, non-threatening tone
   - Maintain distance, non-confrontational posture
   - Listen actively
   - Acknowledge feelings
   - Offer choices
   - Set clear limits

### Medications (if verbal fails)

**Oral** (preferred if patient willing):
- **Risperidone** 2 mg PO
- **Olanzapine** 10 mg PO (or ODT - orally disintegrating)
- **Lorazepam** 1-2 mg PO
- Combination: Antipsychotic + benzodiazepine

**IM** (if oral refused):
- **Haloperidol** 5 mg IM + **Lorazepam** 2 mg IM (traditional "5 and 2")
- **Olanzapine** 10 mg IM (do NOT combine with IM benzodiazepine - respiratory depression)
- **Ziprasidone** 10-20 mg IM
- **Lorazepam** 2 mg IM alone

**Avoid**:
- IM olanzapine + IM benzodiazepine (respiratory depression)
- Typical antipsychotics in Parkinson's, Lewy body dementia

### Physical Restraints (Last Resort)
**Indications**: Imminent danger after verbal and medication failed

**Procedure**:
- 5-point restraints (each limb + chest)
- Continuous observation
- Regular reassessment (q15min)
- Release ASAP
- **Document thoroughly** (medical necessity, alternatives attempted)

**Complications**: Asphyxiation, aspiration, rhabdomyolysis

## 📚 Must-Know for Shelf Exam

✓ **NMS**: FEVER (Fever, Encephalopathy, Vital signs, Elevated CK, Rigidity); stop antipsychotic, dantrolene/bromocriptine
✓ **Serotonin syndrome**: Clonus, hyperreflexia, hyperthermia; onset in hours; stop serotonergic drugs
✓ **NMS vs. Serotonin**: NMS = lead-pipe rigidity, days onset; Serotonin = hyperreflexia/clonus, hours onset
✓ **Lithium toxicity**: Tremor, ataxia, confusion → seizures; hemodialysis if severe
✓ **TCA overdose**: QRS widening; treat with sodium bicarbonate; lethal in overdose
✓ **Anticholinergic**: "Blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, red as a beet, hot as a hare, dry as a bone"
✓ **Catatonia**: Lorazepam challenge; treat with benzos or ECT
✓ **Delirium**: Acute, fluctuating, inattention; treat underlying cause; haloperidol if needed
✓ **Delirium vs. Dementia**: Delirium = acute, fluctuating, altered consciousness
✓ **Acute agitation**: Verbal de-escalation first; then PO meds; then IM; restraints last resort

---

**Key Principle**: Safety first, treat underlying cause, minimize interventions to least restrictive necessary.
