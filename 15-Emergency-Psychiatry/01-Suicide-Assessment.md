# Suicide Assessment and Management

## 📊 Epidemiology

### General Statistics
- **10th leading cause of death** in US overall
- **2nd leading cause** in ages 15-34
- **~45,000 deaths per year** in US
- **1.4 million attempts** per year
- **Completion rate**: ~1 in 25-30 attempts

### Key Demographic Patterns

**Completion (Death by Suicide)**:
- **Male > Female** (3-4:1 ratio)
- **White > Other races** (highest in Native Americans and Whites)
- **Elderly** (highest rate in men >65, especially >80)
- **Methods**: Firearms (most lethal), hanging, poisoning

**Attempts**:
- **Female > Male** (2-3:1 ratio)
- **Younger age groups** (15-24)
- **Methods**: Overdose, cutting (less lethal)

## 🎯 Risk Factors

### Major Risk Factors (Highest Yield)

#### Demographic
- **Male gender** (completion)
- **Age >45** or **adolescent**
- **White or Native American**
- **Divorced, widowed, single**
- **Social isolation**
- **Unemployed**

#### Psychiatric
- **Previous suicide attempt** (strongest predictor!)
  - 30-40% of completers had prior attempt
  - Risk highest in first year after attempt
- **Psychiatric disorder** (present in >90% of completers):
  - **Major Depression** (15% lifetime risk)
  - **Bipolar Disorder** (15-20% lifetime risk - HIGHEST of all disorders)
  - **Schizophrenia** (10% lifetime risk)
  - **Substance Use Disorders**
  - **Personality Disorders** (especially Borderline)
  - **Eating Disorders**
  - **PTSD**
- **Recent psychiatric hospitalization**
  - Highest risk: **First week after discharge**
- **Comorbid substance use**

#### Clinical Presentation
- **Hopelessness** (strongest psychological predictor)
- **Severe anxiety, panic attacks**
- **Psychosis** (command hallucinations)
- **Impulsivity**
- **Agitation**
- **Insomnia**
- **Recent significant loss** (relationship, job, health)

#### Current Episode
- **Suicidal ideation with plan**
- **Intent to act**
- **Access to lethal means** (especially firearms)
- **Preparations** (giving away possessions, writing note, updating will)
- **Rehearsal** of suicide attempt

#### Medical
- **Chronic pain**
- **Terminal illness**
- **Recent diagnosis of serious illness**
- **Functional impairment**

#### Recent Events
- **Discharge from psychiatric hospitalization** (first week highest risk)
- **Starting antidepressant** (black box warning, especially age <25)
- **Recent loss** (death, divorce, job)
- **Legal problems**
- **Financial crisis**
- **Humiliation, shame**

### Protective Factors

- **Strong social support**
- **Children in the home** (especially young children)
- **Responsibility to family**
- **Religious beliefs** (many religions prohibit suicide)
- **Positive therapeutic relationship**
- **Access to mental health care**
- **Effective coping skills**
- **Problem-solving skills**
- **Reasons for living**
- **Future orientation**
- **Pregnancy**

## 🗣️ Assessment - What to Ask

### Direct Questions (Always Ask Directly!)

**Screen**:
1. "Have you been feeling down or depressed?"
2. "Have you had thoughts that life is not worth living?"

**Assess Ideation**:
3. "Have you had thoughts of hurting yourself or ending your life?"
4. "Are you having these thoughts right now?"

**Assess Plan**:
5. "Have you thought about how you would do it?"
6. "Do you have [method mentioned]?" (access to means)
7. "Have you taken any steps toward this?" (preparation)

**Assess Intent**:
8. "Do you intend to act on these thoughts?"
9. "What's stopping you from acting on these thoughts?" (protective factors)
10. "On a scale of 0-10, how likely are you to act on these thoughts?"

**Assess Past Attempts**:
11. "Have you ever tried to hurt yourself or end your life before?"
12. "When was that? What happened?" (method, medical severity, circumstances)

**Clinical Note**: Asking about suicide does NOT increase risk. It's essential to ask directly.

## 📋 Risk Stratification

### High Risk (Immediate Intervention Required)

**Features**:
- Current suicidal ideation with **specific plan and intent**
- **Previous attempt** (especially if recent or medically serious)
- Access to **lethal means**
- **Active preparation** (writing note, giving away possessions)
- **Psychotic symptoms** (command hallucinations)
- **Severe hopelessness**
- **Agitation, severe anxiety**
- Recent **significant loss**
- **No protective factors**

**Management**:
- **1-to-1 observation** (constant monitoring)
- **Psychiatric hospitalization** (voluntary or involuntary)
- Remove all potentially dangerous items
- No bathroom/shower alone
- Consult psychiatry immediately

### Moderate Risk

**Features**:
- Suicidal ideation without specific plan or intent
- OR vague plan without intent
- Previous attempt (remote)
- Psychiatric disorder present
- Some protective factors present
- Willing to engage in safety planning

**Management**:
- **Close monitoring** (q15-30 min checks)
- **Psychiatric consultation**
- Consider hospitalization vs. intensive outpatient
- **Safety plan** (crisis numbers, removing means)
- Close follow-up (within 24-48 hours)
- Involve family/support system

### Low Risk

**Features**:
- Fleeting thoughts without plan or intent
- Strong protective factors
- Good insight
- Willing to contract for safety
- Engaged in treatment
- No recent attempt

**Management**:
- **Outpatient treatment**
- Safety planning
- Close follow-up (within 1 week)
- Emergency contact information
- Assess for changes

## 📝 Safety Planning

Essential components of a safety plan:

1. **Warning signs**: List thoughts, feelings, behaviors that indicate crisis
2. **Internal coping strategies**: Things patient can do alone (exercise, relaxation)
3. **Social contacts for distraction**: Friends, family (not necessarily about crisis)
4. **People to ask for help**: Trusted contacts who can help in crisis
5. **Professionals to contact**: Therapist, psychiatrist, crisis line
6. **Emergency contacts**:
   - **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (call or text 988)
   - Local crisis line
   - Emergency department
7. **Means restriction**: Remove or limit access to lethal means
   - Firearms (most important - remove from home)
   - Medications (lock up, limited quantities)
   - Sharp objects
   - Rope, other ligatures

## 🏥 Management

### Immediate Interventions (High Risk)

1. **Ensure Safety**:
   - Constant 1-to-1 observation
   - Remove dangerous items (belts, shoelaces, sharps, medications)
   - Safe environment (locked unit if hospitalized)

2. **Psychiatric Hospitalization**:
   - **Voluntary**: Patient agrees to admission
   - **Involuntary**: If patient refuses but meets criteria
   - Criteria for involuntary: Danger to self, danger to others, or gravely disabled
   - Typical hold: 72 hours (varies by state)

3. **Treat Underlying Condition**:
   - Depression → Antidepressant (monitor closely, black box warning)
   - Psychosis → Antipsychotic
   - Severe agitation → Benzodiazepine PRN
   - Substance intoxication/withdrawal → Manage appropriately

4. **Involve Family/Support**:
   - With patient permission (unless emergency)
   - Educate about warning signs
   - Ensure safe environment at home

### Outpatient Management (Lower Risk)

1. **Safety Planning** (as above)

2. **Means Restriction**:
   - **Firearms**: Single most important intervention
   - Work with family to remove from home
   - Medications: Dispense limited quantities

3. **Frequent Follow-up**:
   - High risk: Daily to every other day initially
   - Moderate risk: Weekly
   - Phone contact between visits

4. **Crisis Plan**:
   - 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
   - Local crisis numbers
   - Emergency department
   - On-call psychiatrist

5. **Treat Underlying Disorder**:
   - Antidepressants (if depression)
   - Psychotherapy (especially CBT, DBT)
   - Treat comorbid conditions

6. **Address Modifiable Risk Factors**:
   - Substance use
   - Insomnia
   - Chronic pain
   - Social isolation

## 🚨 Special Populations

### Adolescents
- **2nd leading cause of death** ages 15-24
- Impulsivity is major factor
- Bullying, academic stress, relationship problems
- Social media impact
- **Screen at every visit** (PHQ-9 modified for teens)
- Involve parents/guardians in safety planning

### Elderly
- **Highest completion rate** (especially men >80)
- Often use **more lethal means**
- Fewer warnings before attempt
- Risk factors: Isolation, chronic illness, functional decline, bereavement
- May present as somatic complaints

### Postpartum
- **Postpartum depression**: 10-15% (screen with Edinburgh Scale)
- **Postpartum psychosis**: 0.1-0.2% - psychiatric emergency
- Risk of infanticide with psychosis
- Thoughts of harming baby → immediate hospitalization

### Substance Use
- Intoxication and withdrawal both increase risk
- Impairs judgment, increases impulsivity
- Treat intoxication/withdrawal
- Address substance use in treatment plan

### Chronic Medical Illness
- HIV/AIDS, cancer, neurological disorders, chronic pain
- Address pain, depression, functional impairment
- Palliative care consultation if appropriate

## 📊 Screening Tools

### Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS)
- Most widely used
- Assesses ideation, intensity, behavior
- Free, brief, validated

### PHQ-9 (Question 9)
- "Thoughts that you would be better off dead or hurting yourself"
- Not comprehensive but good screen

### Beck Scale for Suicide Ideation (BSS)
- 21 items
- More detailed

### SAD PERSONS Scale (Less Favored Now)
- **S**ex (male)
- **A**ge (<19 or >45)
- **D**epression
- **P**revious attempt
- **E**thanol/substance abuse
- **R**ational thinking loss (psychosis)
- **S**ocial support lacking
- **O**rganized plan
- **N**o spouse
- **S**ickness (chronic/terminal)

**Note**: SAD PERSONS has fallen out of favor; clinical judgment more important than numerical scores

## 🎓 Clinical Pearls

✓ **Always ask directly** - does NOT increase risk
✓ **Best predictor of future behavior is past behavior** (previous attempt)
✓ **Hopelessness** is strongest psychological predictor
✓ **Male gender** = higher completion rate (more lethal means)
✓ **First week after discharge** from psychiatric hospital = highest risk period
✓ **Starting antidepressant** = increased risk first 4 weeks (black box warning, age <25)
✓ **Bipolar disorder** has highest lifetime suicide risk (15-20%) of major psychiatric illnesses
✓ **Remove firearms** - single most important means restriction
✓ **988** - New suicide & crisis lifeline (call or text)
✓ **Safety planning** should be collaborative with patient
✓ **Contract for safety** alone is NOT sufficient (not evidence-based)
✓ **Involuntary commitment** when danger to self and refuses voluntary admission
✓ **Psychotic depression** with command hallucinations = very high risk
✓ **Chronic suicidality** (personality disorders) managed differently than acute risk
✓ **Document thoroughly**: Risk factors, protective factors, reasoning for disposition

## ❓ Common Exam Scenarios

**Scenario 1**: 25-year-old man with depression, says "life isn't worth living"
- **Action**: Ask directly about suicidal ideation, plan, intent, past attempts

**Scenario 2**: Patient endorses suicidal ideation with plan to shoot himself, has gun at home
- **Answer**: High risk; psychiatric hospitalization (voluntary or involuntary), remove firearm

**Scenario 3**: 80-year-old man with terminal cancer asks about assisted suicide
- **Action**: Assess for depression (treatable), explore concerns, palliative care consult; distinguish from suicidal ideation

**Scenario 4**: Patient discharged from psychiatric hospital 3 days ago, now returns with suicidal ideation
- **Answer**: Very high risk (post-discharge period); readmit to hospital

**Scenario 5**: Adolescent with wrist cuts, says "I just wanted attention"
- **Answer**: Take seriously (all self-harm is concerning); assess suicide risk; may indicate distress even if not suicidal intent

**Scenario 6**: Patient with borderline personality disorder, chronic suicidal ideation without plan
- **Answer**: Different management (DBT, validation, boundaries); hospitalize only if acute change or high-risk behavior
